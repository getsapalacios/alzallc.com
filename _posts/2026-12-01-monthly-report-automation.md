# How to produce your monthly owner or business report in minutes instead of a weekend

The month ends. Now someone has to build the report. You export from three systems, copy numbers into a spreadsheet, fix the formatting, write a short summary, and send it. If you manage property, you do it once for every owner.

For many small businesses, this takes a full weekend every month. The work is not difficult. It is just slow, and easy to get wrong when you are tired.

This post shows how to turn your monthly report into a routine with much less hand work, and how to choose a way to get there.

## Why monthly reporting takes so long

Most reports take hours for the same reasons:

- **The numbers live in different places.** Your accounting tool, your property or job software, your bank, and a spreadsheet or two.
- **Someone copies them by hand.** Every copy-and-paste is a chance for a mistake.
- **The format changes every month.** Without a fixed template, each report gets rebuilt from scratch.
- **The summary is written fresh.** Someone stares at the numbers and tries to explain what changed.
- **It is never anyone's first job.** So it gets pushed to evenings and weekends.

Here is an illustrative example. A property manager with 12 owners spends 45 minutes to 1 hour per owner report. That is 9 to 12 hours every month. *(Illustrative numbers. Time your own next report to find out.)*

## What an automated report does

A good automated report does the same five steps you do today, without the manual work:

**1. Pulls the numbers on a set day.** On the 1st of the month, it collects last month's figures from the tools you already use.

**2. Puts them into one fixed template.** The same layout every month: income, expenses, open items, and anything you track, such as occupancy, jobs completed or overdue invoices.

**3. Checks for problems.** If a number looks wrong, such as a missing deposit or an expense far above normal, the report flags it for you instead of hiding it.

**4. Drafts a short plain-language summary.** For example: "Rent collected was 98% of expected. One repair was approved. Two leases renew next month." You read it and edit if needed.

**5. Waits for your review.** Nothing goes out until you approve it. Then each report goes to the right person.

## What to include in a monthly owner or business report

Keep it short. Most owners and business partners want the same few things:

- **Money in.** Rent or revenue collected compared with what was expected.
- **Money out.** Major expenses, with anything unusual called out.
- **Open items.** Late payments, open repairs, unpaid invoices.
- **What is coming next month.** Renewals, planned work, known costs.
- **A 3-sentence summary.** What went well, what needs attention, what you recommend.

If your report has these five parts in the same order every month, your readers will understand it in under 2 minutes.

## How to choose a way to get the report off your plate

There are several ways to stop building the report by hand. Each has a real place.

**1. Reporting or accounting software.** Your property management or accounting software may already have report templates. If it does, use them. The gap is that most reports pull from more than one place, and the software only knows its own numbers. Someone still has to set up the template, add the outside figures, write the summary and check for mistakes. In one survey of software buyers who regretted a purchase, 33% said it cost more than expected and 32% blamed slow or hard implementation (Gartner Digital Markets, 2023).

*Software is the right choice when* your numbers mostly live in one system and its built-in report covers what you need.

**2. A virtual assistant or office admin.** A person can pull the numbers and build the report. You still train them, check their work, and start over if they leave.

*A person is the right choice when* the report changes a lot each month and needs judgment on every line.

**3. An agency or freelancer to set it up.** You get a working report without learning the tools. The risk is the handoff. When one of your apps changes how it exports data, the report can break, and someone has to own the fix.

*An agency is the right choice when* you have staff who can maintain the report afterward.

**4. A done-for-you service.** Someone sets up the report and keeps it running for you. You give up some control and rely on the provider.

*A done-for-you service is the right choice when* you want the report handled and have no one on your team to own it.

*Watch for:* ask who fixes it when an export changes, and what you keep if you leave.

**5. Keep doing it yourself.** With a fixed template and the checklist below, it gets faster. It still takes your evenings.

*Doing it yourself is the right choice when* you send only one or two short reports a month.

## What to ask about cost

Prices change, so ask every option these questions before you decide:

- What is the monthly price, and is there a setup fee?
- Are texting, email or extra users charged on top?
- Who fixes it when an export changes or something breaks, and is that included?
- What do you keep if you leave?

## What should always stay with a person

The report can be built automatically. The judgment should not be:

- Reviewing every report before it is sent
- Explaining a bad month or an unexpected cost
- Any recommendation that involves money, such as raising rent or approving a large repair
- Answering follow-up questions from owners or partners

Automation removes the copying and formatting. You keep the part that builds trust: your review and your explanation.

## A simple checklist to start this month

You can do this before you automate anything:

1. **Time your next report.** Write down how long it takes from first export to final send.
2. **List your sources.** Every place a number comes from.
3. **Make one fixed template.** Use the five parts above.
4. **Pick a send date.** For example, the 5th of every month.
5. **Write down your checks.** What would make you stop and look twice? A missing payment? An expense over a set amount?

Once you have a fixed template, a list of sources and a set of checks, you have everything needed to automate the report.
